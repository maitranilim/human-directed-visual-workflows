# Human-Directed Visual Workflows

I use this repository to keep track of how I direct AI image work: what I asked to preserve, what changed, what failed, and what I would reuse. The images were generated or edited with AI. My work was in the brief, constraints, critique, selection, and organization.

![Selected visual experiments](assets/gallery-overview.jpg)

## Start with the evidence

- [Selected experiments](experiments/README.md) show the images and the questions behind them.
- [Preservation locks](prompt-library/preservation-locks.md) spell out what an edit must leave alone.
- [The delta-edit loop](workflows/delta-edit-loop.md) shows how I repair one failure at a time.
- [The failure catalog](failures/README.md) records recurring mistakes and fixes.
- [My contribution](docs/HUMAN-CONTRIBUTION.md) describes my role and the limits of the record.

The method is simple: define the job, lock the parts that matter, make an edit, inspect the result, and keep the fragment that worked. I started documenting it because one-shot prompts made good results hard to repeat.

## What this does and does not show

There are examples across photo edits, posters, branding, wallpapers, and interface concepts. Personal photos and third-party meme templates are excluded from the public sample.

I have observed fewer unnecessary regenerations while using this approach, but I do not have reliable historical timing or cost data. The [measurement protocol](benchmarks/measurement-protocol.md) is for collecting that evidence going forward.

AI image tools did the image generation and most of the editing. What's mine is the direction and the judgment; I'm not claiming I illustrated these by hand.
